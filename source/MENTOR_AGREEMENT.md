# Hera — Mentor Confidentiality, Boundaries and Conduct Agreement

**Version 2026-10-07.1 · Effective 9 October 2026**

This agreement is for Hera mentors. You accept it before your mentor application can be submitted, and it applies for as long as you are listed as a mentor. It sits alongside the Terms of Use, the Privacy Notice and the Community Guidelines, which also apply to you as a member.

Thank you for offering to do this. What follows is short on purpose.

---

## 1. What you are

You are a **volunteer peer mentor**: a mother offering support from your own lived experience. You are not a Hera employee, agent, contractor or representative, and Hera does not pay you.

Being listed as a mentor is not a professional qualification and does not mean Hera has verified your background, training or history.

---

## 2. Peer support only

- **No diagnosis.** Never tell a mother what condition she has or might have.
- **No treatment or medical advice.** Never advise on medication, feeding decisions with a medical dimension, a treatment plan, or whether to seek, stop or change medical care. You can always say what you did and what happened to you.
- **No therapy or counselling.** Do not offer, describe or imply a therapeutic relationship, even if you are professionally qualified elsewhere. You are here as a peer.
- Share experience, not instructions. "This is what helped me" is right. "You should do this" is not.

If you are a qualified health professional, that does not change your role here, and you must not present yourself as acting in that capacity through Hera.

---

## 3. Confidentiality

What a mother tells you through Hera is hers, not yours.

- Do not repeat, forward, quote or summarise it to anyone else — not to other mentors, not to friends, not publicly, not anonymously.
- **Do not take screenshots** of what she shares, and do not copy it out of the app.
- Do not post about a mother you are supporting in the Circle, even without naming her. Details that feel general are often recognisable.
- Do not keep your own record of her personal information.

Two limits, and you should tell a mother about them if it becomes relevant: you may pass information to the Hera team where you are reporting a safety concern (section 6), and Hera may access information where it is necessary to respond to a report or meet a legal obligation.

---

## 4. Boundaries

- **Keep it on Hera.** Do not ask for, offer or accept a phone number, personal email, social media account or any other contact detail, and do not arrange to meet. Hera does not support or permit off-platform contact in this version. If a mother offers her details, decline kindly and say why.
- **No romantic or sexual conduct**, no flirtation, and no comments about a mother's body beyond the ordinary experience of postpartum recovery she has raised herself.
- **No harassment**, pressure, repeated contact after someone withdraws, or attempts to reconnect after a connection ends.
- **No money.** Do not ask for or accept payment, gifts, loans or donations, and do not enter any financial arrangement with a mother you meet through Hera.
- **No promotion.** Do not sell anything, recruit for anything, advertise a business or service — your own or anyone else's — or use Hera to build a client base.
- **No proselytising.** Do not press your religious, political or ideological views.
- **Be honest about your availability.** You are not obliged to be available or to reply. It is far better to say "I can manage a message every few days" than to disappear.

---

## 5. Crisis and safety

**You are not a crisis service and you must not act as one.**

If a mother tells you she is in immediate danger, or that she is thinking of harming herself, her baby or someone else:

1. **Tell her to contact emergency services now** — 995 for an ambulance, 999 for the police in Singapore — or to go to the nearest A&E.
2. Stay warm and stay present if you can, but **do not take responsibility for her safety** and do not agree to be the person who keeps her safe.
3. Do not promise to keep something confidential when someone's safety is at risk.
4. **Report it to the Hera team** by emailing heraformothers@gmail.com as soon as you reasonably can.

Hera does not monitor conversations and no one at Hera is watching in real time. Reporting a safety concern to Hera is **not** an emergency response and never substitutes for calling emergency services.

If a mother's situation is beyond peer support — and much of it will be — the most useful thing you can do is say so kindly and point her to the Support tab.

---

## 6. Telling us about concerns

Report anything that worries you, including safety concerns, behaviour that breaks the Community Guidelines, someone asking you for contact details or money, or a situation you feel out of your depth in. Email the Hera team at heraformothers@gmail.com — Report in a conversation's menu shows the address. You will not get in trouble for reporting something in good faith.

If you are struggling yourself, you are a Hera member too. Step back, pause your availability, or end a connection — none of that requires an explanation.

---

## 7. Ending a connection

Either you or the mother can end a connection at any time, for any reason or none. You do not owe anyone an explanation, and there is no penalty. You can also turn off new requests, or stop being listed as a mentor, whenever you want.

---

## 8. Removal

Hera may decline your application, pause your listing, end a connection, or remove you as a mentor at any time — including without notice where someone's safety may be at risk. Serious breaches of this agreement may also mean your Hera account is suspended or terminated under the Terms of Use.

---

Anything at all: **heraformothers@gmail.com** — moderation and safety lead, **Hera Moderation & Safety Lead**.
