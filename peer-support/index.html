You are shown this notice once, just before you send your first mentor request. It sets out what peer support at Hera is, and what it is not. It adds nothing to your agreement with Hera: every point below restates Terms of Use, section 8 and the Mentor Agreement, which are the documents that govern mentorship.

What a mentor is

Hera mentors are volunteer mothers who have been through pregnancy or early motherhood and have offered to listen. They are approved by Hera before they are listed, and they accept the Mentor Agreement covering confidentiality, boundaries and conduct.

A mentor shares her own experience. That is the whole of what she offers, and for many mothers it is enough.

What a mentor is not

  • A mentor is not a doctor, nurse, midwife, psychologist, counsellor or social worker acting in that capacity — even where she happens to hold such a qualification.
  • A mentor cannot diagnose a condition, treat one, or advise you on medication, feeding, or any other medical decision.
  • A mentor is not trained to respond to a crisis, and is under no obligation to be available at any particular time.
  • Nothing a mentor says is medical advice. Where your health or your baby's health is concerned, speak to a healthcare professional.

Hera is not an emergency service

No one at Hera is monitoring the app for people in danger. Messages are not read as they are sent, and no alert reaches any person or service because of something you write. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, contact emergency services directly — in Singapore, 995 for an ambulance or 999 for the police. Further numbers are listed on our Support page.

What you choose to share

When you send a request, a mentor sees your display name, your profile photo if you have one, your motherhood stage, the age of your youngest child, the support you are looking for, the time you hope to have each week, and what you write to her. Share only what you are comfortable sharing, and take the time you want before sharing more. You are never obliged to disclose a diagnosis, your address, your workplace, or anything else about your family.

Mentors agree to keep what you tell them private. That is a promise Hera holds them to, but it is a promise made by a volunteer, not a legal duty of confidence owed by a clinician.

If something feels wrong

Trust that feeling. You can end a mentorship at any time, without giving a reason and without telling the mentor why.

Report a mentor to us from the Mentor tab, or email heraformothers@gmail.com. Reports reach the Hera Moderation & Safety Lead and are handled inside Hera; they are not passed to any third party. We can suspend or remove a mentor, and we will tell you what we have done.

The full terms

These points are stated in full in Terms of Use, section 8 and in the Mentor Agreement. Where this summary and those documents differ, those documents apply.

Hera is an unincorporated, student-led project in Singapore providing non-clinical peer support. It is not a medical or emergency service.
